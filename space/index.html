How it works

select_repos      Jev scores every repo for "is this claimable"; no Groq tokens
fetch_facts       deterministic: README, tree, commits, tests, CI, dependencies

Why claims survive or die

verify_claims is plain Python. Every digit in a generated claim must appear in that repository's evidence bundle, and every supporting quote must be both present and relevant to the claim it backs. A claim that fails is not softened — it is dropped, and reappears as a measurement task.

The clearest example from a real run: the model wrote "losing 745 out of 1484", having computed 1484 &minus; 739 itself. The evidence contains 739 and 1484 but never 745, so the claim was refused.

Measured quality

Claim faithfulness — the text that actually reaches a resume — and number precision both measure 1.00. Whole-summary faithfulness is 0.83, and that gap is reported rather than averaged away: the descriptive problem and approach fields are framing, are not run through verify_claims, and can contain fair-sounding inference.

Reproducibility, measured

Two independent runs over the same repositories, both at temperature: 0:

Half of the pipeline Result
Verification (verify_claims) Identical. Same 102/111 verdicts, the same 6 rejections, the same reasons.
Generation (build_resumes) Variable. ATS scores moved by up to 24 points (balanced 59.1 → 34.8).

That split is the design working as intended, not an accident: the half that decides what may be said is deterministic Python, so it cannot drift. The half that decides how to say it is a language model, and Groq's gpt-oss models are not reproducible even at temperature 0.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: treat a single ATS score as indicative, not precise. What is stable is which claims are allowed through — and that is the property the project actually promises.

Operating inside a free tier

The binding constraint is 8,000 tokens per minute, not the request rate. One evidence-linked repository summary costs about 2,074 tokens, so the ceiling is roughly four per minute.
