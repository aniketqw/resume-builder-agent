=====================================================================
EVALUATING THE v2 RESUME-BUILDER ATS SCORER ON PUBLIC BENCHMARKS
A literature-survey style write-up
=====================================================================
Status: COMPLETE for scoring-vs-labels (all three datasets, full sets)
and for the edit experiments on cnamuangtoun and 0xnbk. The edit
experiments were NOT run on netsol (see 6.3).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THE ATS SCORER IS (what was happening in our resume builder)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The scorer (v2/src/rba/ats/, v2/src/rba/resume/) takes a resume and a job
description (JD) and returns one number. It uses NO language-model call at
scoring time; it is arithmetic, so the same input always gives the same
score. It fuses three independent "channels" with a weighted sum:

  Channel   Weight  What it measures
  -------   ------  --------------------------------------------------
  Dense     0.5     Semantic similarity. The text is turned into a
                    vector by the BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 model (run via
                    ONNX) and compared to the JD vector by cosine.
                    Long resumes are cut into chunks (one per section or
                    entry) and the chunk vectors are averaged, because
                    the model only reads about 512 tokens at a time.
  Lexical   0.2     BM25 keyword overlap between resume and JD, scored
                    against a background corpus of other job texts.
  Skills    0.3     Weighted coverage of the JD's skills by the
                    resume's skills, using a curated alias table
                    (e.g. "JS" = "JavaScript").

If a channel is unavailable (for example the JD names no known skill),
its weight is shared across the others rather than counted as zero.
The weighted sum (a "convex combination") is chosen over Reciprocal Rank
Fusion following Bruch et al., "An Analysis of Fusion Functions for
Hybrid Retrieval" (arXiv 2210.11934), as cited in the project's own
ANALYSIS.md. The overall design follows the second-place system at the
TalentCLEF 2026 resume-ranking task, per the project's own notes.

Per-bullet feedback. The app shows, for every bullet, "how much would
my score change if this line were removed". This is exact leave-one-out:
remove the bullet, re-score, subtract (resume/attribution.py). Bullets
whose removal RAISES the score are flagged as harmful.

Known limitation, stated in the project's own code: the number is
NOT calibrated against real hiring outcomes. No public English dataset
has real hiring outcomes, so the score is a similarity measure, not a
probability of getting an interview.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT WE SET OUT TO TEST, AND WHY
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Two questions:

  Q1 (agreement). Do the scorer's numbers agree with human/LLM-judged
     fit labels on public resume-JD datasets?
  Q2 (editing). When the app says "adding or removing this bullet
     helps", is that actually true, and does it help for the RIGHT
     reason (relevance) rather than a side effect (length, keywords)?

Public datasets have no "before and after an edit" labels, so Q2 is
answered with controlled experiments: edit a real resume, re-score,
and compare with the unedited version (a paired design).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE DATASETS: WHAT THEY ARE AND HOW RELEVANT THEY ARE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
All three were downloaded and inspected before use. Counts below are
what we actually loaded, not just what the dataset cards claim.

3.1 0xnbk/resume-ats-score-v1-en  -- PRIMARY
  - 6,374 resume+JD pairs (5,099 train + 1,275 validation).
  - Stored as ONE text column; resume and JD are joined by the literal
    marker " SEP " (exactly one per row). We split on it.
  - Labels: a continuous ATS-style score (19.2 to 90.1) and a tier:
    No Fit 3,206 / Potential Fit 1,592 / Good Fit 1,576.
  - Mean label score by tier: No 24.9, Potential 54.8, Good 84.9.
  - Why relevant: its label is the same kind of thing our scorer
    outputs, so rank correlation is a meaningful test.
  - Caveat: labels are LLM-generated/synthetic, not recruiter outcomes.

3.2 cnamuangtoun/resume-job-description-fit  -- PRIMARY (second opinion)
  - 8,000 pairs (6,241 train + 1,759 test), 3-class labels:
    No Fit 4,000 / Potential Fit 2,000 / Good Fit 2,000.
  - Only 345 unique job descriptions; most JDs appear ~10 times with
    different resumes. This is useful for our edit experiments (many
    resumes per JD) but means the JD variety is small.
  - OVERLAP WARNING: about 48% of 0xnbk's resumes (3,066 of 6,374) also
    appear here. The two datasets are NOT independent evidence.

3.3 netsol/resume-score-details  -- PARTLY RELEVANT
  - About 1,031 JSON files: match (648), mismatch (201), and several
    deliberately broken "invalid"/"gibberish" inputs (~140).
  - Each has a resume, a JD, and GPT-4o rubric scores per criterion.
  - Loads only by reading the raw JSON (the standard loader fails).
  - Use: check ordering match > mismatch > invalid. The per-criterion
    rubric cannot be compared because our scorer has no criteria.
  - Privacy: files contain real-looking names/contact details; the
    harness never reads those fields.

Considered and NOT used (and why):
  - resume-ner-bert-v2: tests entity extraction (NER); our scorer does
    no NER. Not relevant to scoring.
  - Kaggle amaymishra11: its "label" is TF-IDF similarity, close to our
    own keyword channel, so agreement would be circular.
  - JobResQA: only 105 synthetic pairs, 5 languages; our embedding model
    is English-only. Too small for accuracy claims; possible later use
    as a fairness (demographic-swap) check.
  - Djinni-CB: relevant in principle (keyword vs holistic reasoning) but
    obtained via a paper, not a simple download. Deferred.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT WE DID (METHOD)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Adapter. Dataset resumes are mostly plain prose without bullet
    markers. v2's own text parser expects bullets, so we split each
    resume into sentence-sized "bullets" (max 60 words) and group them
    six to an entry. Grouping matters: without it the dense channel
    would see only the first 512 tokens. All scoring still goes through
    v2's real scoring functions, not a re-implementation.

4.2 Skills channel without an LLM. The datasets provide no structured
    skills. We take every skill the JD names from v2's alias table and
    treat them all as "required". Consequence: the skills channel works
    only for JDs that mention known tech skills; for others it drops out
    (see "skills_channel_coverage" in results: 64-67% of pairs had it).

4.3 Part A: agreement with labels. Score every pair, then measure:
    Spearman/Kendall rank correlation with the continuous label, AUC for
    tier pairs (Good vs No Fit etc.), tier means, and macro-F1 with score
    cut-offs fitted on a random half and tested on the other half.
    Compared against baselines: TF-IDF cosine and raw bge-small cosine,
    and an ablation of each channel. Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals.

4.4 Part B: bullet-edit experiments (paired, same resume before/after):
  E1 Dose-response. Add k = 1..5 bullets to weak (No/Potential Fit)
     resumes. "Relevant" bullets come from a Good-Fit resume for the same
     JD. "Irrelevant" bullets come from a Good-Fit resume for a different
     JD, matched to the same length.
  E2 Gap-closing. Add 5 same-JD good-fit bullets to a Potential-Fit
     resume; measure the share of the score gap to that good-fit resume
     recovered.
  E3 Pruning. Remove k = 1, 3, 5 bullets: ones the scorer flags as
     harmful, random ones, or the ones it rates most helpful.
  E3b Planted bullets. Hide 3 relevant and 3 irrelevant bullets in a
     resume; can the per-bullet numbers find them (AUC; 0.5 = chance)?
  E4 Budget. Keep only B = 6/10/15 bullets chosen by the scorer's
     best-first selection vs random vs original order.
  E5 Gaming control. Add one fake "bullet" made only of the JD's most
     frequent words; compare with a genuine relevant bullet.
  E6 Fidelity. The app's per-bullet delta vs an independent re-score.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT WE EXPECTED TO SEE (the "should be" observation)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
For a scorer that really measures fit to a job:
  A1. Scores rise with the label: No Fit < Potential < Good, with a
      clear rank correlation to the dataset's own score, and better
      than a plain TF-IDF baseline.
  A2. Adding relevant bullets raises the score steadily with k.
  A3. Adding irrelevant (other-job) bullets changes it little or lowers
      it. The GAP between the relevant and irrelevant curves is the
      evidence that the score tracks relevance.
  A4. A keyword-stuffed non-sentence should NOT beat a real, relevant
      bullet.
  A5. Removing flagged-harmful bullets raises the score; removing random
      ones is about neutral or slightly negative; removing the most
      helpful ones hurts most.
  A6. Per-bullet numbers identify planted relevant bullets as helpful
      (AUC well above 0.5) and planted irrelevant ones as harmful.
  A7. Per-bullet deltas equal an independent re-score.
  A8. Best-first selection at a bullet budget beats random selection.

6.2 Scoring vs labels (Part A)  -- COMPLETE, full datasets
    Spearman = rank correlation with the dataset's continuous score
    (1.0 perfect, 0 none). AUC = chance a higher-tier pair outscores a
    lower-tier one (0.5 = coin flip, 1.0 perfect). 95% intervals are
    all under +/-0.03 for the two large datasets.

    0xnbk (6,374 pairs)       Spearman   AUC Good-vs-No  AUC Good-vs-Pot
      lexical (BM25) only      0.19        0.65            0.57
      dense only               0.28        0.70            0.55
      dense + lexical          0.29        0.71            0.56
      FULL 3 channels (prod.)  0.14        0.59            0.49
      baseline TF-IDF cosine   0.24        0.68            0.58
      baseline raw bge cosine  0.29        0.71            0.56
    Mean raw score by tier (full): No 0.473, Potential 0.507, Good 0.505.
    Tier cut-offs fitted on half the data and tested on the other half:
      macro-F1 0.37 (full) to 0.43 (dense+lexical); always guessing the
      biggest class gets 50% accuracy, and the scorer gets 45-46%.

    cnamuangtoun (8,000 pairs; no continuous label, so no Spearman)
      lexical 0.63 | dense 0.68 | dense+lexical 0.69 | FULL 0.59 |
      TF-IDF 0.66 | raw bge 0.69   (all AUC Good vs No Fit)
      Good vs Potential: full 0.50, dense+lexical 0.54.

    netsol (991 usable files: 648 match, 201 mismatch, 142 invalid)
      Spearman vs its GPT-4o macro score: dense+lexical 0.48, TF-IDF 0.47,
      full 0.39, raw bge 0.34.
      "match" vs "mismatch" AUC: dense 0.71, dense+lexical 0.71, full 0.57.
      Invalid inputs (e.g. a gibberish job description) scored HIGHER
      than valid ones: AUC of match-vs-invalid is only 0.20-0.23, i.e.
      well BELOW chance. Mean lexical score: invalid 0.73 vs match 0.13.
      Cause not yet confirmed; likely rare made-up words get a very high
      keyword weight. Needs a dedicated check.

  A5 harmful>random>helpful       +1.13/-1.08/-4.53 +1.36/-0.83/   MET
                                                      -4.07
  A6 planted bullets identified   0.60 / 0.42       0.64 / 0.43    NOT MET

  A8 selection beats random       50.0 vs 42.6      54.4 vs 47.0   MET

New, larger finding from Part A (6.2) that the edit tests did not show:
  - The scorer's AGREEMENT with the labels is weak. Best Spearman is
    0.29 (0.48 on netsol). The most informative single channel is the
    dense one; plain TF-IDF and a raw embedding cosine do about as well.
  - The full three-channel score, which is what the product shows, is
    WORSE than dense+lexical on every dataset (Spearman 0.14 vs 0.29 on
    0xnbk; AUC Good-vs-Potential 0.49, i.e. no better than a coin flip).
    So the skills channel (30% of the score) is lowering agreement with
    these labels, not raising it. Caveat: our skills input here is a
    crude stand-in (every skill the JD names counts as required), so
    this may understate the channel when real extracted skills are used.
  - The separation between tiers is small in absolute terms: mean raw
    score 0.47 / 0.51 / 0.51 for No / Potential / Good. Potential and
    Good are not separated at all.
  - On netsol, invalid inputs (gibberish JD) get HIGH scores. A scorer
    used for gatekeeping would pass garbage.

Reading in plain terms:
  - The scorer is INTERNALLY CONSISTENT. Its leave-one-out numbers are
    exact, its "remove this" advice really does raise the score, and its
    best-first selection beats random.
  - It does NOT yet clearly separate RELEVANT from IRRELEVANT content
    when text is added. Unrelated bullets raised the score as much as
    relevant ones. With the skills channel removed the gap reverses
    slightly in favour of relevant bullets (0.92 vs 0.68 at k=5), so the
    skills channel looks like a main source of the problem: the other
    resume's bullets name skills, and the JD-skill list is broad, so
    skills credit can come from content that is off-topic overall.
    This is a hypothesis; we have not yet isolated it with a skills-off
    run of every experiment.
  - A bullet made only of keywords can beat a genuine bullet, so the
    score can be gamed by keyword stuffing.
  - Per-bullet helps/hurts numbers measure "does this bullet raise the
    scorer's number", which is not the same as "is this bullet about the
    right job" (planted-bullet AUC near or below chance).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THREATS TO VALIDITY (read before quoting any number)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Labels are synthetic/LLM-generated. Agreement with them means the
    scorer ranks like the labeler, not that a recruiter would agree.
  - 0xnbk and cnamuangtoun share ~48% of resumes: not independent.
  - cnamuangtoun has only 345 unique JDs, so JD variety is limited.
  - Our adapter splits prose into sentences; real resumes with real
    bullets and dates may behave differently.
  - Our "required skills = every skill the JD names" is a crude
    stand-in for real extraction, and may inflate the skills channel.
  - 100 targets per experiment and one random seed: wide confidence
    intervals on small effects (E1 at k=1 especially).
  - "Irrelevant" bullets come from Good-Fit resumes, so they are
    well-written resume text. That may make them look better to the
    dense channel than a typical random bullet would.
  - These tests cannot say whether a score gain helps in a real hiring
    pipeline. A small hand-labelled set of real resumes reviewed by
    recruiters is still the recommended final check.

9. IMPROVEMENTS WE BUILT AND TESTED (opt-in, existing code untouched)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
All fixes live in one new module, v2/src/rba/ats/improved.py. No existing
module imports it (a unit test enforces this), every fix is off by default
(Fixes()), and Fixes.all() turns them on. Benchmark code: extract_skills.py,
run_improved.py, analyze_improved.py, run_edits.py --scorer baseline|L|R.
LLM use: JD text only (public data), local llama3.1 via Ollama (cloud models
timed out); resumes are never sent to an LLM.

P1 LLM-extracted JD skills + deterministic match in the resume text.
P2 Top-k dense chunk pooling.        P3 Keyword-stuffing guard.
P4 Input-validity guard + capped BM25 IDF for unseen words.
P5 Requirement-by-requirement matching.

9.1 Agreement with labels (60 JDs per dataset; ~1,300 pairs each)
  Baseline -> fix:
  - P1: 0xnbk Spearman 0.149 -> 0.257, AUC Good-vs-No 0.62 -> 0.73;
        cnamuangtoun AUC 0.56 -> 0.69; netsol Spearman 0.35 -> 0.40.
        CLEAR WIN. Coverage of LLM skills: 100% / 71% / 99%.
  - P2: no change.  P3: no change (affects only 3/3/68 pairs).
  - P4: neutral on 0xnbk and cnamuangtoun; netsol AUC Good-vs-No
        0.19 -> 0.90 (guard rejected 107/875 netsol pairs, 1/1385 0xnbk).
        CAVEAT: the same-text rule and thresholds were tuned on netsol, so
        this is not evidence for unseen data.
  - P5: did not help (alone Spearman 0.03 on 0xnbk; fitted weight 0).
  - All fixes with weights fitted on 0xnbk (dense .71, lexical .19,
    skills .10): not better than P1 alone (0.231 vs 0.257).

9.2 Edit experiments (cnamuangtoun, 81 pairs, same targets for all)
                          baseline   L (P1)    R (recommended set)
  +5 relevant bullets       +4.26    +2.13     +2.13
  +5 irrelevant bullets     +3.54    +1.33     +1.35
  relevant / irrelevant      1.2x     1.6x      1.6x
  stuffed fake bullet       +0.73    +0.82     +0.04
  genuine bullet            +1.10    +0.55     +0.62
  gap closing (median)       78%      10%       28%
  Reading: the stuffing guard (P3) works. Irrelevant bullets still raise
  the score, but the gap to relevant ones widened (mostly from P1). The
  old gap-closing figure was inflated by the skills shortcut.
  Not run: E3 pruning and E4 budget under the fixed scorers; netsol edits.

9.3 Where do irrelevant-bullet gains come from? (channel isolation)
  Same targets, R scorer restricted to one channel (cnamuangtoun, k=5):
                   relevant  irrelevant  ratio
  baseline           +4.26     +3.54      1.2x
  R (all channels)   +2.08     +1.28      1.6x
  dense only         +1.55     +0.93      1.7x
  keyword only       +0.65     +0.30      2.2x
  skills only        +3.92     +2.51      1.6x
  Finding: irrelevant text raises the score in EVERY channel, so it is not
  one channel's bug. Keyword matching separates best (2.2x) but moves
  little. The remaining lever is a relevance gate on the gain itself, not
  a channel swap. (Survey step 2b, "skills off", is now answered.)
  Note: the R row in 9.2 above used a variant that also had top-k dense
  pooling; the re-run recommended set without it (this table) gives
  +2.08 / +1.28 / stuffed +0.04, gap-closing median 6% (top-k variant 28%).
  Top-k pooling therefore did help gap closing, while changing no
  label-agreement metric; it was removed to keep the code small and can
  be restored from git history (commit 86721d9).

9.4 Calibration and fitted weights (0xnbk)
  Monotone (isotonic) map raw score -> label score, fitted on half of the
  JDs, tested on the other half: mean error 20.2 label points vs 22.3 for
  always predicting the mean. A real but small gain: the score is still a
  rough guide, not a probability. Weights fitted on 0xnbk (dense .81,
  keyword .11, skills .08): 0xnbk Spearman 0.242 (vs 0.258 default), netsol
  0.460 (vs 0.395): mixed, so the defaults were kept.
  Dense alone: Spearman 0.229, AUC Good-vs-No 0.709, close to the full
  recommended set (0.258 / 0.727): the skills channel adds little here.

9.5 Verdict and what shipped in this branch
  Shipped as opt-in in src/rba/ats/improved.py: P1 LLM skills, P3 stuffing
  guard, P4 validity guard + capped BM25 (Fixes.recommended()), plus
  fit_isotonic / calibrate. Removed: P2 top-k pooling, P5 requirement
  matching. Existing scorer files are untouched. The v1 prototype was
  deleted from this branch (still in git history); the secret-scan script
  moved to scripts/ and CI now runs the secret scan and v2 tests only.
  Limits: small subsets, one dataset for edits, one seed, local llama3.1.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
10. SUGGESTED NEXT STEPS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Investigate why the skills channel lowers agreement (Part A) and
     test with properly extracted skills (Ollama/Groq) instead of the
     "every JD skill is required" shortcut.
  2. Investigate the gibberish-JD result on netsol (suspected rare-word
     inflation in the keyword channel) and add an input-validity guard.
  2b. Run E1/E5 with the skills channel fully off in every experiment
     (so far only the sensitivity curves were), to confirm where the
     irrelevant-bullet gain comes from.
  3. If confirmed, consider a relevance-aware skills credit (only count
     skills the JD asks for AND that appear in context), and a guard for
     bullets with no sentence structure (the keyword-stuffing case).
  4. Add a hand-labelled real-resume check.
